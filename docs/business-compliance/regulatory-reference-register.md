# Regulatory Reference Register

Verified against primary government sources: 2026-10-14

## Use and ownership

This register is a starting control index, not a universal clause list or legal determination. The contracting lead owns solicitation/award applicability. The export lead owns jurisdiction, classification, license, end-use, end-user, and destination analysis. The import lead owns entry, classification, value, origin, admissibility, and partner-government-agency requirements. Counsel or a qualified specialist reviews uncertain, high-risk, defense, sanctioned, or novel transactions.

Review this register quarterly and before relying on it for a new solicitation, award, country, party, item, technical-data transfer, or regulatory change. Save the source URL, access date, version/date, reviewer, and decision in the applicable record.

## Evidence quality standard

Every compliance decision should record:

- record ID and `correlationId`;
- legal entity and organization scope;
- transaction facts and assumptions;
- exact source, source version/date, and access timestamp;
- reviewer and independent approver where required;
- decision, rationale, conditions, expiration/review date, and attachments;
- unresolved red flags and hold status;
- change history without overwriting the earlier decision.

Automated or AI-generated summaries are drafts. Only an authorized human may set a regulated gate to `CLEARED`, and only after reviewing the underlying evidence.

## Excluded-by-default ITAR and controlled-scope boundary

ShieldMate does not plan to operate an ITAR-controlled defense-trade program. That business decision is a scope control, not a jurisdiction finding, registration exemption, license, or safe harbor. No document in this repository asserts DDTC registration, CMMC certification, or an approved controlled cloud environment.

Allowed workflow statuses are:

| Status | Meaning | Allowed transition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `STANDARD_COMMERCIAL_REVIEW` | Intake appears commercial but required facts and ordinary trade review are not complete. | `EXPORT_CLASSIFICATION_REQUIRED`, `HOLD_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW`, or `APPROVED_COMMERCIAL_SCOPE` |
| `EXPORT_CLASSIFICATION_REQUIRED` | Jurisdiction or classification evidence is missing or facts suggest a controlled item, software, technology, or service. | `HOLD_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW`, `APPROVED_COMMERCIAL_SCOPE`, or `REJECTED_CONTROLLED_SCOPE` after qualified human review |
| `HOLD_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW` | Facts, screening, jurisdiction, authorization, data handling, party, destination, or end-use issue is unresolved. | Remain held, or move only after an authorized documented resolution |
| `REJECTED_CONTROLLED_SCOPE` | Activity falls outside ShieldMate's approved commercial scope or cannot be lawfully/safely handled. | Closed; a new materially different intake requires a new record |
| `APPROVED_COMMERCIAL_SCOPE` | An authorized reviewer documented that the proposed scope may continue through ordinary contract/trade gates. | The next applicable compliance gate; this is not shipment clearance |

Intake must record product name and technical characteristics; jurisdiction/classification status where needed; manufacturing country; destination and intermediate destinations; end user and intended end use; defense-article, defense-service, or technical-data indicators; restricted-party/sanctions results; and whether users could upload controlled technical data.

Set `HOLD_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW` immediately for ambiguity. Set `REJECTED_CONTROLLED_SCOPE` and prevent upload, disclosure, bid, transfer, shipment, or service when facts indicate apparently ITAR-controlled activity, classified data, unauthorized export-controlled technical data, prohibited parties/destinations, or another restricted activity outside the approved environment. Preserve only the minimum non-controlled refusal/audit metadata; do not copy suspect controlled content into the tracker, repository, Slack, AI prompts, or unfinished TradeOps services.

Only a qualified human reviewer may make or approve a binding jurisdiction, classification, licensing, exemption/exception, sanctions, or disclosure decision. AI and MCP output may identify missing facts and draft a review packet but cannot issue that decision. See [BIS jurisdiction guidance](https://www.bis.gov/licensing/determine-what-is-subject-to-the-ear), [BIS classification guidance](https://www.bis.gov/licensing/classify-your-item), [DDTC public portal](https://www.pmddtc.state.gov/ddtc_public), and [22 CFR Parts 120-130](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-22/chapter-I/subchapter-M).

## Sanctions and restricted-party screening procedure

Use this manual, phased procedure until a separately approved provider and integration exist:

1. Collect legal names, known aliases, addresses, countries, identifiers, ownership/control facts, roles, destination, end user, intermediate parties, and end use for all material parties.
2. Screen against the current applicable official lists. Use the [Trade.gov CSL](https://www.trade.gov/consolidated-screening-list), the [OFAC Sanctions List Service](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-list-service), [OFAC Search](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-list-search-tool), SAM exclusions when relevant, and program/list-specific official sources.
3. Capture source/list, URL or dataset identifier, version or retrieval timestamp, search terms, match settings, analyst, result, and preserved evidence.
4. Treat every candidate as `POTENTIAL_MATCH`; place the transaction on hold for manual review.
5. Evaluate name/alias, identifiers, address/location, ownership/control (including applicable OFAC ownership rules), sanctions program, list-entry details, end use, destination, and the restriction's exact terms.
6. Record an authorized disposition, rationale, conditions, reviewer, date, and evidence. Never treat a fuzzy score as an approval.
7. Rescreen before execution and on relevant party, ownership, destination, routing, end-use, source-list, or timing changes.

Resolution states are `NO_CANDIDATE_MATCH`, `POTENTIAL_MATCH`, `FALSE_POSITIVE_REVIEWED`, `CONFIRMED_RESTRICTION`, `INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION`, and `LEGAL_REVIEW_REQUIRED`. Only `NO_CANDIDATE_MATCH` or an authorized `FALSE_POSITIVE_REVIEWED` disposition may continue to the next independent gate. A clean name search is not blanket clearance of ownership, destination, product, end use, license requirements, or transaction structure.

Official machine-readable options include Trade.gov CSL CSV/TSV/JSON and API data and OFAC SLS downloadable datasets. Trade.gov describes the API as an open machine-readable aid with fuzzy matching, requires users to consult the official source/list restriction when a candidate appears, and disclaims endorsement of a score in its [API terms](https://developer.trade.gov/terms-of-service). OFAC SLS is the primary official download surface for current SDN and non-SDN data. Capture source timestamps and request/response evidence; do not build automatic clearance or release behavior.

### Provider evaluation, not selection

| Cost tier | Expected method | Evaluation criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Official-source/manual (no data-license fee; staff and legal-review cost remains) | Low-volume searches and downloaded official data | Current-list coverage, reproducibility, evidence capture, trained review, ownership analysis, and rescreen capacity |
| Low-cost commercial | Named-user web search and basic reports | All official-source criteria plus aliases/transliteration, audit exports, support, retention, privacy, and contract terms |
| Mid-tier API/batch | Batch/API screening with case management | Data freshness, matching quality/tuning, API limits, stable identifiers, ownership data, test/sandbox support, versioning, SSO/RBAC, audit logs, and false-positive workflow |
| Enterprise/global trade platform | Multi-jurisdiction lists, continuous monitoring, workflow, and enterprise controls | Coverage, explainability, legal updates, uptime/SLA, data residency, security assessment, integrations, segregation of duties, retention/export, implementation and exit costs |

Obtain comparable written quotes before assigning dollar estimates; usage, party volume, lists, ownership datasets, continuous monitoring, seats, and integrations materially affect price. Provider selection remains an owner decision after the internal low-volume method is evaluated.

## Customs broker and importer-of-record boundary

ShieldMate should initially use independent CBP-licensed customs brokers when complexity warrants professional brokerage. A broker acts under appropriate authority but does not erase the importer's duty to exercise reasonable care or responsibility for entry facts, duties, taxes, and compliance. ShieldMate must not offer unlicensed customs business, file through another customer's identity, or activate production customs-submission automation.

| Responsibility | Required designation/evidence | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Purchaser/owner | Legal entity, purchase order, title/risk terms | Does not automatically make ShieldMate the IOR for a customer's goods |
| Importer of record | `Importer_Of_Record`, `IOR_Eligibility_Basis`, importer number held outside this template, and `Responsible_Party_Approval` | One eligible party; ShieldMate only for its own eligible, approved imports |
| Ultimate consignee | Named recipient, address, eligibility, delivery evidence | Must not be used to disguise the actual purchaser/IOR |
| Exporter/USPPI where applicable | Named entity, authority, EEI responsibility | Determine separately under the FTR; Incoterms do not decide it |
| Foreign supplier | Legal name, address, manufacturer relationship, origin support, invoice | Supplier statements require validation |
| Carrier/freight forwarder | Named provider, route, authority, transport evidence | Freight forwarding is not importer approval or customs clearance |
| Customs broker | `Customs_Broker_Name_or_Not_Used` and `Broker_License_Verification` | Use a permitted broker; the broker and importer communicate directly for customs business |
| Broker power of attorney | `Broker_POA_Reference`, scope, signer authority, effective/termination dates | Keep sensitive POA separately; do not expose it in a general tracker |
| Customs bond | `CBP_Bond_Responsibility`, bond type/status/reference | Confirm responsible principal and adequate bond before entry when required |
| Duties/taxes/fees and entry data | `Duties_and_Taxes_Responsibility`, valuation, entry instructions, reconciliation | Paying a broker does not remove the importer's liability |
| HTS/origin/PGA | `HTS_Classification`, country-of-origin evidence, marking, admissibility, PGA determinations | Qualified review; procurement origin and customs origin are distinct analyses |
| Shipment acceptance/records | `Customs_Entry_Reference`, `Commercial_Invoice`, `Shipment_and_Delivery_Evidence`, discrepancies, retention trigger | Retain entry records for the applicable period; five years is the general CBP baseline, subject to longer duties/holds |

If no qualified IOR or necessary authorized broker arrangement is documented, set `HOLD_COMPLIANCE_REVIEW` and do not accept or release the shipment. Primary guidance: [CBP broker overview](https://www.cbp.gov/trade/programs-administration/customs-brokers), [CBP broker-use guidance](https://www.help.cbp.gov/s/article/Article-1169?language=en_US), [CBP importing guide](https://www.cbp.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Importing%20into%20the%20U.S.pdf), [CBP entry recordkeeping](https://www.help.cbp.gov/s/article/Article1840), and [19 CFR Part 163](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-19/chapter-I/part-163).
